World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine and Russia agreed in Abu Dhabi to swap 1,000-1,500 prisoners per side, the war's largest (NPR). Iran said Hormuz stays shut pending seven answers; its navy reported three tankers struck mines and caught fire, unconfirmed (Al Mayadeen). Russia launched 163 drones, hitting six power substations; 135,000 consumers in Odesa lost power, its governor said (ISW). Drones struck Russia's Ust-Luga oil port, setting it ablaze and injuring three workers (Reuters). A 41-year-old US soldier was arrested, uncharged, at NATO's Imjin Barracks for suspected spying for Russia (BBC). Israeli forces killed at least seven in Gaza, a father and 13-year-old son among them (Reuters). NATO sent jets up after four or more drones crossed into Baltic airspace (Reuters). Guterres told COP31 that plans imply warming past 2.5°C, as five nations pledged to double homes with rooftop solar to 300 million by 2030 (UN News; AP). China's trade surplus reached $1.04 trillion in ten months; October's was $105.3 billion (Reuters).

Mississippi's Senate seat went to Democrat Scott Colom by 4,669 votes, the first Black senator there since Reconstruction (AP). The House sent Hegseth's impeachment to committee on a 211-204 vote (Reuters). Three Iran-backed operatives were charged by the DOJ over nearly 20 attacks, including a September plot against RAF Fairford (Times of Israel). Bessent, the Treasury chief, moved to a military base after death threats from Iran (NYT). The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.03% as October US prices rose 0.4%, core 0.3% (BLS; CNBC). The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in Congo hit 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths (AP). At Abiemnom, South Sudan, UN peacekeepers failed to stop a March massacre killing 213, Amnesty said (AP). Israel's Knesset opened with Netanyahu's bloc on 53 seats and Eisenkot's on 48 (Reuters). The Privy Council struck down Trinidad and Tobago's 1986 ban on private, consensual same-sex relations, 5-0 (AP).
