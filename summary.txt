World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi with no ceasefire and no deal on territory, but agreed a large prisoner exchange that US envoy Jared Kushner called the biggest release of the war; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive and quite substantive" (NPR). NATO scrambled jets overnight after at least four drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier in England on suspicion of spying for Russia.

Iran said the Strait of Hormuz, shut for more than 250 days, will not reopen until the US answers its seven conditions for peace talks, security chief Mohsen Rezaei said; three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris the US economic campaign was devastating enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer" (New York Times).

At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, UN Secretary-General António Guterres told world leaders the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction"; the two-day leaders' summit is expected to bring together 102 heads of state and government (AFP). Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu backed a declaration to double homes with rooftop solar by 2030.

In Gaza, Israeli forces killed at least seven people, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said, as the truce frayed. In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it, and the Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over alleged plots in the US and Europe. The Democratic Republic of Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths in the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak, the second largest on record.
